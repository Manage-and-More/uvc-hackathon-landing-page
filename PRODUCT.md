## Platform

## Stack

Next.js. Chosen by the user for the option to extend into a full-stack app later (participant applications are a plausible future addition, not a current requirement). Today the surface is a single marketing page.

## Users

The page is a partner-recruitment asset, not an application funnel. Three audiences, all sent the link directly rather than arriving via search:

1. **UVC portfolio company operators** (founder / CTO / C-level) being asked to own a challenge track. They are deciding whether a weekend of their team's attention and a cash contribution returns real work on a real problem, plus brand exposure to strong builders.
2. **UVC partners and principals** being asked to lend the fund's name and take jury seats. They raised the open questions this page has to answer; they are assessing whether the organizers can deliver at the promised quality level.
3. **A frontier AI lab** being approached as compute partner. Currently a prospect, not a partner.

**Not the current audience:** participants. Builders are the eventual audience of a later surface; nothing on this page needs to convert them.

## Product Purpose

A two-day on-site hackathon in Munich on **agentic AI for industry**, organized by Manage & More (UnternehmerTUM's flagship entrepreneurship program) together with UVC Partners (UnternehmerTUM Venture Capital Partners).

The page exists to make the concept legible and credible enough that: portfolio companies commit challenge tracks, UVC partners commit jury seats, and an AI lab opens a compute-partner conversation. Success is signed partners, not traffic.

It also functions as the answer to UVC's standing list of open questions about format, structure, sponsorship, prizes, and readiness. Where an answer is not yet decided, the page shows the decision as open rather than inventing one.

## Positioning

**A hackathon built around a venture fund rather than around a corporate sponsor.**

The VC is the structure, not a logo in a footer. Its portfolio companies set the challenge tracks, so the problems worked on are live commercial problems with an owner in the room. Its partners sit on the jury, so participants get evaluation from people who value startups for a living. The fund gets concentrated exposure to strong technical builders and a look at how they work under pressure.

A corporate-sponsored hackathon cannot offer investor access. A generic student hackathon cannot offer real production problems. This format is the only one that offers both, and neither neighbor can truthfully copy it.

**Unstated ambition:** this is designed as the first instance of a repeatable format, intended to run with other funds after this one. That ambition should be legible in the craft and confidence of the page. It must not be stated as a claim, and no other fund may be named.

## Operating Context

- Munich, Germany. Two-day on-site format: kick-off Saturday morning, final demos and jury Sunday evening.
- Target scale roughly 150 builders, dependent on venue.
- Four challenge tracks: one corporate partner track, two UVC portfolio startup tracks, one track built on a UVC investment hypothesis.
- Jury composition: a CTO or C-level representative from each startup partner, one UVC partner, one seat for the corporate partner.
- Judging criterion that defines the event: **agents that actually run**, not the pitch alone. Teams are evaluated on working software.
- Compute partner supplies AI credits for building across the weekend and as prizes.
- The event is a joint Manage & More × UVC production. Both organizations sit under UnternehmerTUM, which is the structural reason the partnership exists.

## Product Principles

1. **Everything on the page is either true today or visibly marked as open.** The readers are investors and operators who will check. One overstated claim costs the partnership; an honest TBD costs nothing.
2. **The fund is the structure, not the sponsor.** Every section should make the VC-centered format legible, because that format is the whole reason this event is worth a partner's money.
3. **Prove capability with track record we actually own, and state it at its true size.** Manage & More's numbers and three Munich hubs at ~100 builders are ours. The global program's scale is not. Understating a real fact is survivable; inflating one in front of investors is not.
4. **Answer the skeptic before they ask.** UVC's open questions — dates, sponsorship structure, prizes, attendance risk, delivery quality — are the real brief. A question left unaddressed reads as a question unconsidered.
5. **Read as the first edition of a format, not a one-off student event.** The ambition to run this repeatedly, with other funds, shows up as craft and confidence. Never as a claim.
